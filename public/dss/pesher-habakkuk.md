# Pesher Habakkuk (1QpHab)

One of the original seven Cave 1 scrolls. Habakkuk 1–2 is quoted and interpreted as referring to the last days, the Kittim, the Teacher of Righteousness, and the Wicked Priest. Biblical lemmas follow the King James sense; the interpretation is rendered for reading in this app. Photographs: Leon Levy Digital Library.

## Column 1–2

*“The oracle which Habakkuk the prophet saw. How long, O Lord, shall I cry, and you will not hear?”* (Habakkuk 1:1–2)

[The beginning of the column is broken.] Its interpretation concerns the beginning of the last generation.

*“For a work is to be done in your days which you will not believe though it be told you.”* (Habakkuk 1:5)

Its interpretation concerns the traitors with the Man of the Lie, for they did not believe the words of the Teacher of Righteousness from the mouth of God; and concerning the traitors to the new covenant, for they were not faithful to the covenant of God, and they profaned his holy name. Likewise, its interpretation concerns the traitors in the last days. They are the ruthless ones of the covenant, who will not believe when they hear all that is to come upon the last generation, from the mouth of the priest whom God placed in the midst of the congregation to interpret all the words of his servants the prophets, by whom God told all that is to come upon his people and his land.

## Columns 3–4 — The Kittim

*“For I am raising up the Chaldeans, that bitter and hasty nation.”* (Habakkuk 1:6)

Its interpretation concerns the Kittim, who are swift and mighty in war, to destroy many. In the counsel of the guilty they walk; with cunning and deceit they deal with all the peoples.

*“They are terrible and dreadful; their judgment and their dignity proceed from themselves.”* (Habakkuk 1:7)

Its interpretation concerns the Kittim, the fear and dread of whom is upon all the nations. In counsel all their thought is to do evil, and with cunning and deceit they deal with all the peoples.

*“Their horses are swifter than leopards, and more fierce than the evening wolves.”* (Habakkuk 1:8)

Its interpretation concerns the Kittim, who trample the land with their horses and with their beasts. From afar they come, from the islands of the sea, to devour all the peoples like an eagle — and there is no satiety. With heat and with wrath, with faces of fury, they speak with all the peoples. For this is what he said: *“They fly as an eagle that hastens to eat. They come all of them for violence; their faces are set forward.”*

*“They mock at kings, and princes are a scorn to them.”* (Habakkuk 1:10)

Its interpretation: they mock at the great and despise the honoured. At kings and princes they scoff, and they mock a great people.

*“They will pass through like the wind and they will transgress and they will make their strength their god.”* (Habakkuk 1:11)

Its interpretation concerns the rulers of the Kittim, who pass by in the counsel of a guilty house. Each, in his turn, his rulers come to destroy. *“They make their strength their god”* — its interpretation: they make their weapons their worship.

## Columns 5–7 — The Teacher of Righteousness

*“You are of purer eyes than to behold evil.”* (Habakkuk 1:13)

Its interpretation: God will not destroy his people by the hand of the nations. Into the hand of his chosen God will give the judgment of all the nations. In their rebuke all the wicked of his people will be guilty — who have kept his commandments in their distress. For this is what he said: *“Too pure of eyes to look on evil.”*

*“Why do you look on the treacherous, and are silent when the wicked swallows one more righteous than he?”* (Habakkuk 1:13)

Its interpretation concerns the house of Absalom and the men of their council, who were silent at the rebuke of the Teacher of Righteousness, and did not help him against the Man of the Lie, who rejected the Torah in the midst of all their council.

*“You have made men like the fish of the sea.”* (Habakkuk 1:14)

Its interpretation: they make men like fish of the sea, to multiply their possessions. *“They catch them in their net and gather them in their drag.”* Its interpretation: they are the Kittim, who gather their wealth with all their booty like the fish of the sea.

*“Therefore he sacrifices to his net.”* (Habakkuk 1:16)

Its interpretation: they sacrifice to their standards, and their weapons of war are the object of their reverence.

*“I will stand upon my watch.”* (Habakkuk 2:1)

Its interpretation: God told Habakkuk to write what was coming upon the last generation, but the fulfillment of the time he did not make known to him.

*“That he may run who reads it.”* (Habakkuk 2:2)

Its interpretation concerns the Teacher of Righteousness, to whom God made known all the mysteries of the words of his servants the prophets.

*“For the vision is yet for the appointed time; it hastens to the end, and it will not lie.”* (Habakkuk 2:3)

Its interpretation: the last time is prolonged, and it is beyond all that the prophets said. For the mysteries of God are wonderful.

*“If it tarries, wait for it, for it will surely come; it will not delay.”* (Habakkuk 2:3)

Its interpretation concerns the men of truth, the doers of the Torah, whose hands do not slacken from the service of truth, when the last time is drawn out for them. For all the times of God will come to their appointed measure, as he decreed for them in the mysteries of his wisdom.

*“Behold, his soul is puffed up; it is not upright in him.”* (Habakkuk 2:4)

Its interpretation: they will double upon them, and they will not find mercy at the time of visitation.

*“The righteous shall live by his faith.”* (Habakkuk 2:4)

Its interpretation concerns all the doers of the Torah in the house of Judah, whom God will deliver from the house of judgment, because of their toil and their faith in the Teacher of Righteousness.

## Columns 8–12 — The Wicked Priest

*“Moreover, wealth is treacherous; a haughty man shall not abide.”* (Habakkuk 2:5)

Its interpretation concerns the Wicked Priest, who was called by the name of truth at the beginning of his standing, but when he ruled in Israel his heart became proud, and he forsook God, and he betrayed the statutes for the sake of wealth. He plundered and gathered the wealth of the men of violence who rebelled against God. He took the wealth of the peoples, adding to himself the guilt of transgression. Ways of abomination he worked, in every unclean impurity.

*“Who enlarges his desire as Sheol.”* (Habakkuk 2:5)

Its interpretation concerns the rulers of the Kittim, who despise the fortresses of the peoples and with mockery laugh at them. With a great people they surround them to capture them. With terror and dread they are given into their hand, to destroy them, because of the guilt of those who dwell in them.

*“Will not all these take up a taunt against him?”* (Habakkuk 2:6)

Its interpretation concerns the priest who rebelled, who […] to […] his statutes, and they will tear him […] in the midst of his people. They will inflict on him judgments of wickedness, and horrors of evil diseases they will do to him, and vengeances on his body of flesh.

*“Because you have plundered many nations, all the remnant of the peoples will plunder you.”* (Habakkuk 2:8)

Its interpretation concerns the last priests of Jerusalem, who gather wealth and unjust gain from the spoil of the peoples. But in the last days their wealth, with their spoil, will be given into the hand of the army of the Kittim. For they are the remnant of the peoples.

*“Because of the blood of man and the violence of the land, the city, and all who dwell in it.”* (Habakkuk 2:8)

Its interpretation: the city is Jerusalem, in which the Wicked Priest did works of abomination and defiled the sanctuary of God. The violence of the land: these are the cities of Judah, where he plundered the wealth of the poor ones.

*“Woe to him who gets evil gain for his house.”* (Habakkuk 2:9)

Its interpretation concerns the house of judgment, whose judgment God will give in the midst of many peoples. From there he will bring it up for judgment, and in their midst he will declare it guilty, and with fire of brimstone he will judge it.

*“You have devised shame for your house, cutting off many peoples, and sinning against your soul. For the stone will cry out from the wall.”* (Habakkuk 2:10–11)

Its interpretation: the stone is the priest who […] Israel […]

*“Woe to him who builds a city with blood.”* (Habakkuk 2:12)

Its interpretation concerns the Spouter of Lies, who led many astray, to build a city of vanity with blood, and to establish a congregation with falsehood, for the sake of its glory — to make many toil in the service of vanity, and to make them pregnant with works of falsehood, that their toil may be for nothing; so that they may come to judgments of fire, because they reviled and reproached the elect of God.

*“For the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the Lord, as the waters cover the sea.”* (Habakkuk 2:14)

Its interpretation: when God gives them into the hand of his elect, then the knowledge of the glory of God will come, and […]

*“Woe to him who makes his neighbour drink, pouring out your wrath.”* (Habakkuk 2:15)

Its interpretation concerns the Wicked Priest, who pursued the Teacher of Righteousness to swallow him up in the heat of his anger, in the house of his exile. At the time of the appointed feast of rest, the Day of Atonement, he appeared to them to swallow them up and to make them stumble on the fast day, their sabbath of rest.

*“You are filled with shame instead of glory. Drink, you also, and stagger.”* (Habakkuk 2:16)

Its interpretation: the priest whose shame was greater than his glory, because he did not circumcise the foreskin of his heart, and he walked in the ways of drunkenness to quench the thirst. But the cup of the wrath of God will swallow him up, adding to […] his shame.

*“The violence done to Lebanon will cover you, and the destruction of beasts which made them afraid, because of the blood of man and the violence of the land, the city, and all who dwell in it.”* (Habakkuk 2:17)

The interpretation of the word: Lebanon is the council of the community, and the beasts are the simple ones of Judah, the doers of the Torah. God will condemn him to destruction, as he plotted to destroy the poor. And as for what he said, *“because of the blood of the city and the violence of the land”*: the city is Jerusalem, in which the Wicked Priest did works of abomination and defiled the sanctuary of God. The violence of the land: the cities of Judah, where he plundered the wealth of the poor ones.

## Column 13

*“What profit is the graven image, that its maker has graven it, the molten image, and a teacher of lies?”* (Habakkuk 2:18)

Its interpretation concerns all the idols of the nations, which they make to serve and to worship. They will not deliver them on the day of judgment.

*“Woe to him who says to the wood, Awake; to the dumb stone, Arise!”* (Habakkuk 2:19)

Its interpretation concerns all the nations who serve stone and wood. On the day of judgment God will destroy all the servants of idols, and the wicked, from the earth.
