# Genesis Apocryphon (1QapGen / 1Q20)

Aramaic rewritten Genesis from Cave 1, one of the original seven scrolls: Lamech, Enoch, Noah, and Abram. English for reading in this app, following the better-preserved columns. Photographs: Leon Levy Digital Library.

## Columns 2–5 — Lamech and the birth of Noah

I, Lamech, was frightened exceedingly. I went to Bath-Enosh my wife, and I said to her, “Tell me, and swear to me by the Most High, the Lord of greatness, the King of all ages, whether the seed is from you, whether the conception is from you, whether the planting is from you, and not from any of the sons of heaven, until you tell me all in truth.”

Then Bath-Enosh my wife spoke to me with much heat, and she said, “O my brother and my lord, remember my pleasure […] the time of love, and my panting in the midst of my breathing. I will tell you all in truth.”

Then I, Lamech, ran to Methuselah my father, and I told him all. I asked him to go to Enoch his father, to learn all from him with certainty, for he is beloved and a friend of God, and with the holy ones is his lot. They make known to him everything.

Methuselah went to Enoch his father, to the ends of the earth. He found him there, and he said to Enoch his father, “O my father and my lord, I have come to you […] Lamech your son has a child who is not like other children. His eyes are as the rays of the sun. His hair […] He was born complete. His father Lamech is afraid.”

Enoch answered and said, “I will tell you, my son, all that is hidden. Go, tell Lamech your son: This child is not from the Watchers or from the holy ones. He is truly his son. Call his name Noah. He will be a remnant for you, for he and his sons will be saved from the destruction of all flesh which is to come upon the earth because of the Watchers and because of their sons. They have corrupted their way upon the earth.”

## Columns 6–12 — Noah

I, Noah, was a man of truth. In all my days I walked in the paths of everlasting truth. The Holy One was with me. […] I took wives for my sons from the daughters of my brother’s sons, and I gave my daughters to the sons of my brother’s sons, according to the custom of the eternal statute.

In the six hundredth year of my life […] the fountains of the great deep were opened, and the windows of heaven. I, Noah, was at the door of the ark […]

After the Flood I went out, I and my sons and my sons’ wives. I built an altar on Mount Lubar. I offered burnt offerings and I blessed the Lord of heaven, who had delivered us.

I saw the land. It was all filled with trees and green things. I praised the Lord of all, who had done kindness with the earth, and who had removed and destroyed from it all who do violence and wickedness and deceit.

Then I, Noah, divided the land among my sons. To Shem I gave the middle of the earth, to Ham the south, and to Japheth the north. I blessed the Lord of heaven who had given me descendants on the earth.

## Columns 19–22 — Abram

I, Abram, dwelt in Hebron. There was a famine in all the land. I heard that there was grain in Egypt. I went to the land of Egypt. I reached the river Karmon, one of the heads of the river of Egypt.

I, Abram, dreamed a dream in the night of my entering into the land of Egypt. I saw in my dream a cedar and a palm. Men came and sought to cut down and uproot the cedar, and to leave the palm by itself. The palm cried out and said, “Do not cut down the cedar, for we are both from one root.” The cedar was left because of the palm, and they did not cut it down.

I awoke from my sleep in the night, and I said to Sarai my wife, “I have dreamed a dream […] they will seek to kill me and to spare you. This is all the kindness you must do with me: in every place where we come, say of me, ‘He is my brother.’ I will live because of you, and my soul will be delivered because of you.”

Sarai wept that night because of my words. Then we went into Egypt, I, Abram, and Sarai, toward Zoan.

I, Abram, remained there five years before I was received into the presence of Pharaoh Zoan. Three men from the nobles of Egypt […] came to me. They asked of me abundance of knowledge and wisdom and truth. I read before them the book of the words of Enoch.

They saw Sarai my wife. She was very beautiful to look upon. They told the king. Pharaoh sent and took her. He saw her and wondered at all her beauty. He took her to be his wife. He sought to kill me, but Sarai said to the king, “He is my brother,” that I might be benefited on her account. I, Abram, was spared because of her. I was not killed.

I, Abram, prayed and entreated and asked for mercy, I and Lot my brother’s son with me, in the night, because Sarai had been taken from me by force.

That night God Most High sent a spirit of affliction, an evil spirit, to afflict him and all his household, a spirit of pestilence. He was not able to approach her, and he did not know her. He was with her two years.

At the end of two years the plagues and the afflictions grew strong against him and against all his household. He sent and called all the wise men of Egypt, and all the magicians, with all the healers of Egypt, if they might heal him of this plague, him and his household. The healer and the magician and the wise man could not rise to heal him, for the spirit afflicted them all, and they fled.

Then Hyrcanos came to me, and he asked me to come and pray over the king, and to lay my hands upon him, that he might live. Lot said to him, “Abram my uncle cannot pray over the king while Sarai his wife is with him. Go, tell the king to send his wife away from him, to her husband. Then he will pray over him, and he will live.”

When Hyrcanos heard the words of Lot, he went and said to the king, “All these plagues and afflictions with which my lord the king is afflicted and punished are because of Sarai, the wife of Abram. Let him send Sarai back to Abram her husband, and this plague will depart from you.”

The king called me and said, “What have you done to me? Why did you say to me, ‘She is my sister,’ and she is your wife? I took her to be my wife. Here is your wife. Take her. Go. Depart from all the cities of Egypt. Now pray over me and over my household, that this evil spirit may be commanded to depart from us.”

I prayed over him, and I laid my hands upon his head. The plague was removed from him, and the evil spirit was commanded to depart from him, and he lived.

The king swore to me an oath that he had not known her, and he gave me many gifts. The king gave her to me, and he appointed men to take me out of Egypt.

I, Abram, went with very great flocks, and with silver and gold. I went up from Egypt. Lot my brother’s son was with me. Lot also had great flocks, and he took a wife from the daughters of Egypt.

I camped in the hill country of Bethel, the place where I had built the altar. I called there on the name of the Lord of the ages, and I praised the name of God, and I blessed God. I gave thanks before God for all the flocks and the good which he had given me, and for all the good which he had done with me, and for his returning me to this land in peace.
